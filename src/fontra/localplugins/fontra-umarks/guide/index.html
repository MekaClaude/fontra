U+Marks

Accented glyphs, built from your anchors.

You draw the letters and the accents. U+Marks does the rest: it puts the accents on the letters by their anchors, writes the accented glyphs into the font, shows you each accent on its letter while you move an anchor, and tells you when an accented glyph has drifted off its anchors.

The panel reads top to bottom, in the order you work. Each line tells you how far along you are and offers one thing to do. Nothing is a gate: what is ready gets done, the rest waits for you.

① Glyph set — what you want to end up with

The menu lists the glyph sets you chose for this font. The first time, it is empty: Add a glyph set… offers your project's glyph sets, your own (the ones you added to Fontra), Fontra's presets (Google Fonts Latin Core, Adobe Latin, Koeberlin…), or From scratch. The line then counts the accented glyphs of that set and the parts they are built from; the rest of the glyph set is none of U+Marks's business.

Edit… lets you add or take out glyphs (type their names, or the characters themselves), compose bases with marks of your own — see « Rules, and where they come from » — or take the glyph set off the menu (nothing is deleted in the font). The … menu exports and imports the rules.

② Draw — what is still missing

The parts the accented glyphs need and the font does not have yet: letters and accents. Add N missing glyphs puts them in the font, empty, with their code point, so you find them in the font overview and draw them in Fontra. The glyphs that exist but are still blank in a source are listed under « still to draw ».

Spacing accents (acute, grave, dieresis…) are not drawn: U+Marks makes them from the combining ones. See « Spacing accents ».

③ Anchors — put the anchors on what is drawn

Anchors are what U+Marks composes with: a letter carries top, bottom, ogonek; an accent carries the matching _top, _bottom, _ogonek. The list of positions says where each anchor goes — the middle of the glyph at the x-height, the baseline, the cap height plus ten units — and the tick says whether it is placed. Place anchors adds the missing anchors to every drawn glyph of the set, in every source, following the italic angle if you ask it to. It never moves an anchor that is already there.

To add or move one anchor by hand, use Fontra: right-click on the canvas, Add Anchor, then drag it. U+Marks reads what you did.

④ Check — are the anchors where they should be?

Check anchors compares the anchors of the set with their positions and lists the ones that are out of place, source by source. An anchor U+Marks placed whose position has since changed — you edited the positions, or the glyph — is « out of place »: Re-place puts it back. An anchor you moved yourself is « moved by hand » and stays where you put it, unless you tick it.

Adjust holds the batch tools: Move, Align, Copy from, Mirror, Derive variants. One operation at a time; the sentence above Apply says exactly what will happen.

⑤ Compose — write the accented glyphs

Every accented glyph of the set has a rule: a letter, one or more accents, each on an anchor — eacute is e + acutecomb @ top. The line says how many are ready, how many wait for a part you have not drawn yet (and which), and how many can't be built yet without a change to a rule or an anchor. Make writes the ready ones: components only, the letter at the origin, each accent anchor to anchor, the advance of the letter, in every source.

A glyph that already has a drawing of its own is « drawn by hand ». Make leaves it alone; tick it and a sheet shows the old and the new, and asks before replacing anything.

⑥ Update — when the anchors have moved

Once a glyph is made, U+Marks keeps an eye on it. Move top on e: the ghosts follow at once, and every glyph built on e has « moved off its anchors »; the line says by how much and in which sources. Update puts the components back on the anchors, and touches nothing else. A component you moved yourself counts too: the glyph tells you, and you choose — Update, or leave it as it is.

Why does the canvas already show à?

With text shaping on, Fontra's canvas composes à on the fly: it reads your anchors, builds mark positioning from them, and puts gravecomb on a. That is a preview, and a good one — it shows your anchors are right. The glyph agrave itself is not in the font until Make writes it; exports, character maps and applications that do not shape need it. ⑤ counts what is still to make, whatever the canvas shows.

The open glyph

Under the path, the glyph you are editing.

On a letter: its ghosts — every accent a rule puts on it, drawn where it would sit, following the anchor as you drag it; the menu shows all of them, or one. Then Composes: the glyphs built on this letter — made ✓, to make ○, or possible but not in your glyph set — click one to add it. And what its anchors would allow beyond Unicode: one click writes the rule.

On an accented glyph: its rule, which you can change; its state; the ghost is what Make would write, so a drift shows at once.

On an accent: the letters it sits on, in ghost.

Rules, and where they come from

A rule is written for you from three sources, in this order:

  • Unicode. The canonical decomposition of each character of the set: é is e + U+0301, so eacute is e + acutecomb @ top. Stacked accents follow (ǘ is u + diaeresis + acute). Soft-dotted letters take their dotless twin for an accent above: imacron is idotless + macroncomb.
  • Your font. An accented glyph that already exists says its own rule: Aacute built from A and acute.cap reads as that, and counts as made.
  • Your anchors. For a glyph Unicode does not know, any letter with top and any mark with _top can be composed. On the open glyph, « What its anchors would allow » lists them; one click writes the rule. In Edit…, « Compose these bases with these marks » writes a whole set at once, named base_mark unless you say otherwise.

A rule written for you is marked auto. Change it, or press Keep, and it is yours: U+Marks never touches it again. Auto rules follow the font — draw acutecomb.cap and the capitals pick it up, and ⑥ tells you which glyphs to update.

Accents by category: a capital takes acute.cap when the font has it, a small capital acute.sc; the table is in the rule's menu. Anchors top.cap and _top.cap work too, for fonts that prefer one accent drawing, placed lower on capitals.

Spacing accents

The spacing accents of Latin-1 — acute, grave, dieresis… — are not drawn. Each gets a rule of its own kind: one component of the combining accent, with an advance computed from the font (the width of the accent plus two equal margins, the margin of your o unless you set another in the settings). They go through ⑤ and ⑥ like everything else.

Undo

Every batch — adding glyphs, placing or re-placing anchors, Make, Update, Adjust, the matrix — is remembered as one. Undo last batch takes the last one back, glyph by glyph, and leaves what you touched since, saying so. Fontra's own ⌘Z still works, one glyph at a time.

Sharing the rules

The rules, the glyph sets you chose, the anchor positions and the settings live in the font (customData, under umarks), so your collaborators see the same list. The … menu of ① exports them as a JSON file, to carry a family's rules to the next, and imports one — yours, or an old MakeAccents file — without ever replacing a rule you wrote by hand.

On Fontra Pak or your own Fontra server

Settings › Plugins, add the address Uplus-Type/fontra-umarks. Fontra loads the latest release from the public repository; nothing to install. In Fontra Hive it is already there.
